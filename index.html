Why I made it

Many farming games are full of machinery and busy schedules. Evergarden is the opposite: a peaceful place where you build a garden from scratch and feel the small wins along the way. It draws on games like Stardew Valley and Minecraft.

The goal

Let players act out the gestures of starting their own farm, as a short, meaningful break in a rural world with simple controls and a relaxing atmosphere.

Who it's for

Younger learners, including K–12 students, and anyone who has never farmed before. Along the way it offers light exercise and real facts about how food grows.

How it plays

Controls

Good to know

  • Look for hints. Look at a tool, the bell or the merchant's booth and a short hint appears.
  • Tools find their way home. Drop a tool and it returns to the tool rack.
  • Rest the soil. Planting the same crop again and again wears the soil out.
  • End the day early. Ring the farm bell. Once every animal is in the barn, the farm skips to morning.

Behind the build

How Evergarden was made, and where it's going next.

Abstract

    How it's made

    Headset
    Meta Quest 3
    Engine
    Unity 2022.3 LTS, written in C#
    Interaction
    XR Interaction Toolkit: grab, pour, pluck and pet
    Planning
    Started from a game design document to shape ideas and the timeline
    Testing
    Weekly mentor reviews and progress reports
    Assets
    3D models from the Unity Asset Store, audio edited in Audacity

    What I found

    VR can offer a real break from fast-paced life. By focusing on comfort and simple, physical interactions, Evergarden lets people try a lifestyle very different from their own and build a calm personal space.
