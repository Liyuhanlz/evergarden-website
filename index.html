Introduction

Virtual reality (VR) adds full-body immersion and hand interaction that PC, console, and mobile games lack, yet few VR games are built for relaxation. Evergarden is a calm VR farm, inspired by Stardew Valley and Minecraft, where players grow a garden from scratch at their own pace. The project draws on computer science, game design, and agricultural education.

Goals

  • Provide a low-pressure, relaxing escape
  • Use intuitive gestures to plant, water, and harvest
  • Encourage light physical activity
  • Lay groundwork for teaching K–12 students about farming

Results

A playable Quest 3 farm: till, plant, water, and harvest carrots, tomatoes, and corn by hand, with a day/night clock, a voiced merchant NPC, and animals to pet and call home.

Good to know

  • Look for hints. Look at a tool, the bell or the merchant's booth and a short hint appears.
  • Tools find their way home. Drop a tool and it returns to the tool rack.
  • Rest the soil. Planting the same crop again and again wears the soil out.
  • End the day early. Ring the farm bell. Once every animal is in the barn, the farm skips to morning.

    Development process

    Each farm tile moves through five states. Each watered day grows the crop; harvesting returns the tile to Tilled.

    1. Grass
    2. Tilled
    3. Planted
    4. Watered
    5. Harvest
    void OnCollisionEnter(Collision c) {
  if (c.gameObject.CompareTag("Hoe"))
    SetState(TileState.Tilled);
}
void OnParticleCollision(GameObject p) {
  if (p.CompareTag("Water"))
    SetState(TileState.Watered);
}

    Evergarden shows that VR's physical interaction can make farming a calm, hands-on experience and a starting point for teaching K–12 students where food comes from.
